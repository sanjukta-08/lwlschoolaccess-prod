Program Vision

The law is a living argument.

It is an argument about power, fairness, and human possibility.

From the courts of ancient Athens to the assemblies of the United Nations, the law has always reflected the societies that write it — and the individuals brave enough to challenge it. The HUGEM Pre-Law Horizons Program invites students into that conversation.

Over six intensive days, students will engage with legal theory, political history, international law, ethics, and public service. Students will hear from guest speakers on the front lines of change.

They will spend a full day giving back to the local community and exploring public interest law. And they will leave having argued their own case for how the law should be different.

“Law is not what lawyers know. It is a question every generation must answer for itself.”

Program Schedule

The six-day docket.

Each day adds a new layer of context before students argue their own proposal on Saturday.

  1. Day 1 · Monday

    Foundations of the Law

    • Introduction to Legal Terminology & Types of Law

      What kind of law am I interested in?

    • Introduction to Political Theory & Legal Grounding

      From ancient Greece to contemporary society — why does law exist?

    • Guest Lecture
  2. Day 2 · Tuesday

    History of the State

    • Types of Government

      What are the differences between democracy, theocracy, and autocracy?

    • History of the United States

      How and when was the U.S. Constitution written?

    • Political Economy

      How are governments connected to the economy?

    • Research Methods Workshop

      How does a lawyer establish facts in a case?

  3. Day 3 · Wednesday

    Cultural Boundaries & International Law

    • Jurisdiction of the United Nations

      What can the UN do when international law is broken?

    • Guest Speaker
    • Media & the Impacts of Globalization

      How does interconnectedness change the law?

    • Sovereignty

      Is state independence possible in a globalized world?

    • Professional Writing Workshop

      How do I write persuasively in an AI-dominated world?

  4. Day 4 · Thursday

    Revolution & Transformation of Power

    • Legal Ethics

      When and how are decisions made about changing the law?

    • Justice

      What is justice, and who is justice for?

    • Law Student Panel

      What are emerging lawyers thinking about?

    • Capstone Project Briefing

      You are a policymaker in your home country. Make one change to the law.

  5. Day 5 · Friday

    Public Service Day

    • Morning — Community Outreach Service Project

      Students partner with a local Cambridge/Boston organization for hands-on volunteer work.

    • Afternoon — Giving Back as an Attorney

      What does it look like to build a career around service rather than status?

    • Public Interest Law & Government Service Panel

      Legal aid, public defense, prosecution, and nonprofit advocacy careers.

    • Workshop — Pro Bono & Community Advocacy

      How do lawyers use their license to serve people who can't afford one?

  6. Day 6 · Saturday · Half Day

    Argue Your Case

    • Capstone Presentations

      Students argue their proposed legal change before a panel.

    • Awards & Certificate Ceremony

      Recognition for outstanding capstone arguments and program achievement.

    • Closing Address
    • Farewell Celebration

Behind the Program

HUGEM — teaching youth the responsibility of global citizenship.

Each year, HUGEM develops programs around the central theme of global citizenship — first, by helping students see how challenges in their own community intersect with the most pressing international issues, and second, by building the skills to act on them.

Leadership workshops, entrepreneurial pitch challenges, and pre-professional conferences combine learning from Harvard Trainers, hands-on problem-solving, and networking with peers — so students embrace their education and future careers with empathy, autonomy, and the ability to make an actionable impact locally, nationally, and internationally.

HUGEM Innovation Challenge (HIC)

Students research, stress-test, and pitch their own products, with personalized feedback from Harvard Trainers in venture capital and frontier technologies.

Workshops & Conferences

Leadership workshops, pitch challenges, and pre-professional conferences designed to build critical thinking and real-world impact.
