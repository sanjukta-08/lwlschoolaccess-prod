1. Executive summary
Learn with Leaders currently sells eighteen programmes across nine web properties. Google treats
those nine as nine unrelated businesses. Each one competes with the others for the same parent
searches, each one carries a fraction of the brand's authority, and none of them is strong enough to
win a competitive term.
The evidence is in Section 2, but the headline is this: 394 live pages were catalogued across the
nine properties, and the whole estate generates roughly 227 organic visits a month. Almost all of it
comes from blog articles on satellite domains that never mention the programmes they were built to
sell.
The crawl also surfaced something a sitemap-based audit cannot see. Four of the seven satellites joinonwards.com, satinstinct.com, purposeplaybook.ai and the non-blog half of sirluthercenter.com serve one identical HTML shell for every URL. Their real content only appears once JavaScript runs.
That is 146 pages, including a 12,215-word student projects page and a 6,738-word library of
published student research, that Google has effectively never read. It is the strongest argument in
this document for the merge: the destination app renders every page on the server.
The three decisions this document asks you to make
1. Which domain is the permanent home. This document assumes learnwithleaders.com, because it
holds the brand rankings, the backlinks and the press coverage. Every mapping table depends on
this choice.
2. What happens to the fifteen products that exist on satellite sites but are not in the eighteenprogramme catalogue - five masterclass funnels, three camps, the pre-med club, the Global Impact
Fellowship, the Moot Court Championship, the Business & Finance Challenge, Onward LaunchPad,
the FinLit Lab and Project Impact.
3. Whether the 21 school and region landing pages on joinonwards.com move with the merge or stay
as a separate sales estate.

What the merge actually does, in plain terms: every old page issues a permanent (301) redirect to its
true equivalent on the new domain. Google reads that as 'this moved forever', transfers the old
page's history to the new one, and within a few weeks swaps the old address out of search results.
Nothing is lost - provided the destination page is genuinely equivalent and genuinely better.

2.4 Finding three: the backlink profiles are mostly spam
Warning - audit links before you inherit them
learnwithleaders.com shows 8,533 backlinks from just 354 domains. The top seven referring domains
are link directories (directory9.biz, prolink-directory.com, aurora-directory.com and similar),
contributing over 6,800 links with exact-match commercial anchors such as 'medical program for high
school' (4,713 links from 2 domains).
sirluthercenter.com has 34 backlinks, and its most common anchor text is literally an advert for a PBN
backlink service.
Practical consequence: the link equity you are consolidating is close to worthless, and some of it is
actively risky. Do the merge for content and clarity, not for links. Run a link audit on the destination
domain before cutover and prepare a disavow file. Do not buy any more links.

2.6 Finding five: the hub has no technical SEO foundation at all
• learnwithleaders.com returns an HTML page for /robots.txt and /sitemap.xml - meaning neither
file exists. Crawlers are navigating it blind.
• This catalogue app (lwlschoolaccess.com) has no sitemap either, and only five of its routes
declare a canonical URL - three of them relative, two absolute, pointing at two different domains.
• Only joinonwards.com publishes an llms.txt - the file AI crawlers read first. Ironically it is the
domain with the least crawlable content to describe.

3. Ground rules for every redirect
These are not preferences. Break any one of them and the merge loses the value it was meant to
protect.
1. 301, never 302. A 302 tells Google the move is temporary and it keeps the old URL indexed
indefinitely.
2. Server-side only. JavaScript redirects and meta-refresh tags are not reliably followed and pass
little or no equity.
3. Page to page, never bulk to homepage. Google discards the history of a redirect whose
destination is not a genuine equivalent. A homepage-dump merge is functionally the same as
deleting the site.
4. One hop. Old URL to final URL directly. Every extra hop loses a little and risks a loop.
5. The destination must be at least as good. If the new page is thinner than the one it replaces,
rankings fall and do not come back. This is the most common way merges fail.
6. Redirects are permanent infrastructure. The old domains must stay registered and pointed for
years, not months. Budget for it.
7. Never redirect a live application or login flow. Move the product first, then the URL.
8. Nothing goes live until the destination is indexable. Confirm no noindex, correct canonical,
present in the sitemap.

The rule that will be tested
Roughly a third of the URLs in this document map to a page that does not exist yet. That is
deliberate: the mapping tables describe the end state. Section 4 lists everything that must be built
first. Do not shortcut it by pointing those redirects at the homepage.

4.1 Why the subject hubs matter more than they look
A satellite homepage does not rank for a cohort. It ranks for a category: 'harvard pre law program',
'finlit', 'future doctors program'. Send that traffic to a single programme page and you narrow it; send
it to the finder and you dump the visitor into a filter UI. A subject hub is the only honest destination the subject's story, who it suits by grade, the programmes inside it, upcoming dates, FAQs, and links
down into each programme page.
Each hub should carry the language its satellite ranked for. /law needs to talk about Harvard pre-law
and mock trial in its own words, because that is the query it inherits.

4.2 The guides library is the real prize
Ninety-three articles were catalogued across the satellites, and they hold effectively all of the
estate's organic traffic. Migrated onto one domain, cross-linked, and each one ending in a relevant
programme call to action, they become the top of the funnel the business currently does not have.
Keep the slugs identical wherever possible so the redirect is a clean one-to-one.

4.3 Two pages that are quietly load-bearing
• /research/published-papers - the library of published student work on sirluthercenter.com. It is
the most concrete proof the business owns, it is what parents forward to each other, and it is
exactly the kind of verifiable fact AI answer engines cite. Rebuild it, do not fold it into a
testimonial carousel.
• /research/topic-finder - an interactive lead magnet that captures intent before a parent is ready to
apply. Redirecting it to a static page throws away the tool and the leads.

10. Where applications should land
Recommendation: one shared intake at /apply on the canonical domain, pre-filled with the
programme and cohort from the URL, with the Calendly advisor booking as the secondary path for
parents who want to talk first.

Why
• Every satellite currently runs its own /apply page. Leads arrive in separate inboxes with no
shared source attribution, so nobody can say which programme page or which article produced
them.
• A parent who reads a programme page on one domain and then lands on an unfamiliar domain
to apply drops out. Same-domain conversion is measurably better and, after the merge, it is free.
• A single form means a single tagged record: programme, cohort, grade, region, referring page.
That is the difference between guessing and knowing which content sells.

Exceptions
• Where a partner legally owns registration - the Yale IR essay competition already routes to a
Google Form owned by YIRA - keep the external link and label it clearly as a partner registration.
• Where a competition has its own judging platform, link out after capturing the enquiry, not before.

Implementation note
A submissions table on Lovable Cloud behind the form makes applications queryable rather than
only emailed - so 'how many Grade 9 medicine enquiries came from the science fair article' becomes
a question with an answer.

Appendix C - Pre-cutover checklist
• ☐ Canonical domain decided and written into every canonical tag on the destination site.
• ☐ sitemap.xml live and submitted; robots.txt references it.
• ☐ All destination pages for this domain live, indexable and richer than the pages they replace.
• ☐ Blog posts for this domain migrated with matching slugs.
• ☐ Old domain verified in Search Console with a 3-month baseline exported.
• ☐ Redirect map reviewed line by line; no destination is the homepage.
• ☐ No redirect chains; every hop tested with curl.
• ☐ Application flow tested end to end on the destination.
• ☐ Login and portal paths explicitly excluded from the redirect map.
• ☐ Google Ads final URLs rewritten.
• ☐ Social bios and brochure links updated.
• ☐ Change of Address submitted.
• ☐ Monitoring calendar set for daily checks over 14 days.
